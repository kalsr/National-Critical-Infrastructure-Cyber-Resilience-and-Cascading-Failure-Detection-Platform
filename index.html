Application Overview

The KNet National Critical Infrastructure Cyber Resilience and Cascading Failure Detection Platform, developed by Randy Singh of Kalsnet KNet Consulting Group, is a decision support application for government agencies and operators of national critical infrastructure. It demonstrates how energy, water, communications, transportation, healthcare, finance, defense, and emergency services assets depend on one another and on shared third party vendors. The platform helps determine whether multiple outages represent independent technical failures, accidental cascading failures, or potentially coordinated cyberattacks. An important analytical safeguard is built into the platform. Simultaneous outages are never automatically classified as cyberattacks without corroborating evidence. When evidence is insufficient, the incident group is classified as Undetermined rather than attributed to an attacker.

What the Application Does

The platform provides an integrated environment for national infrastructure risk assessment, dependency analysis, incident correlation, and cascading failure detection. It includes an Executive Dashboard with a national risk map, Dependency Graph and Knowledge Graph Explorer, Incident Correlation and Attribution, AI Anomaly Detection, Cascade Simulation Digital Twin, Third Party Vendor Concentration Analysis, Explainable Risk Scoring, Response and SOAR Playbooks, Executive Reporting, Data Management, and Audit Logging. It identifies single points of failure, ranks infrastructure assets on a risk scale from zero to one hundred, and simulates how an individual failure could spread across sectors and states. It also recommends the five assets where additional protection could produce the greatest reduction in potential population impact. The platform identifies hidden dependencies between different infrastructure sectors that rely on the same vendors. Each module supports exporting its results, methodology, field definitions, and formulas into PDF, Word, text, or CSV formats.

How It Works

Users can operate the platform using built in synthetic data containing five fictional scenarios with known outcomes, or upload authorized records in CSV, Excel, or JSON formats. The system first constructs an infrastructure dependency network connecting assets with their suppliers. It then compares asset telemetry against normal operating behavior and identifies potential cyber warning signs, including unusual failed login activity and unexpected outbound data transfers during the twenty four hours preceding an incident. The platform groups related incidents by timing, dependency relationships, shared vendors, and geographical location. Each incident group receives an evidence weighted probability assessment. Indicators such as threat intelligence matches, malware signatures, and unauthorized configuration changes increase the likelihood of malicious activity. Physical failures, weather conditions, and scheduled maintenance activities can reduce that likelihood. A cyber assessment requires at least two independent categories of corroborating evidence. Analysts document their final determinations with written justification, while platform activities such as logins, uploads, approvals, and exports are recorded in an audit trail.

Data and Evidence Handling

The platform supports synthetic demonstration data as well as authorized infrastructure records uploaded by users. It analyzes asset dependencies, supplier relationships, incident timelines, operational telemetry, geographical information, and potential indicators of malicious activity. Evidence is evaluated through an evidence weighted approach that distinguishes confirmed indicators from possible warning signs. The platform does not automatically attribute incidents to cyber attackers based solely on simultaneous outages. When supporting evidence is insufficient, the assessment remains Undetermined. Analysts provide written justification for final determinations, and system activities are maintained through an audit log. These capabilities support traceability, accountability, and more defensible incident assessments.

Tools and Technologies Used

The application is developed using Python and Streamlit, providing an interactive browser based interface. NetworkX performs dependency graph analytics, including betweenness centrality, PageRank, and articulation point analysis to identify critical infrastructure dependencies and single points of failure. Scikit learn Isolation Forest provides machine learning based anomaly detection. Pandas and NumPy support data processing and analytical calculations, while Monte Carlo methods power cascading failure simulations. Plotly and Matplotlib generate interactive charts and maps, and Graphviz creates process and dependency diagrams. ReportLab and python docx support automated PDF and Word report generation. The application also maps relevant behaviors to MITRE ATT&CK for ICS techniques, includes an offline map of the United States, and provides role based access control for viewers, analysts, and administrators.

Real World Benefits

The platform helps government agencies such as DHS, CISA, DOE, FAA, and DoD, along with critical infrastructure operators, identify vulnerabilities before they develop into major disruptions. It helps organizations prioritize limited resilience funding toward the infrastructure assets where additional protection can provide the greatest benefit. Its evidence based analytical approach reduces the risk of incorrectly attributing outages to cyberattacks, helping prevent unnecessary public concern and unwarranted escalation. It also helps analysts distinguish potential cyber incidents from accidental or physical failures during rapidly developing emergencies. Sector specific response playbooks provide prioritized response guidance, including recommended coordination with partner agencies and Information Sharing and Analysis Centers. Response actions remain subject to human approval. Executive reports support leadership briefings, preparedness exercises, resilience planning, and post incident reviews.

Security and Demonstration Notice

This application is a demonstration build intended to illustrate national critical infrastructure dependency analysis, cyber resilience assessment, incident correlation, and cascading failure simulation. The vendors, infrastructure assets, and events included in the demonstration are fictional. Built in demonstration sign in passwords must be replaced with properly secured user accounts and authentication mechanisms before any operational deployment. The platform should undergo appropriate security testing, access control validation, and authorization reviews before use with operational infrastructure information. It should not be treated as an accredited government system or connected to classified infrastructure in its current demonstration form.

Overall Value

The KNet National Critical Infrastructure Cyber Resilience and Cascading Failure Detection Platform provides a unified analytical environment for understanding infrastructure dependencies, identifying vulnerabilities, detecting potential cyber anomalies, and evaluating cascading failure scenarios. By combining Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Graph Analytics, Knowledge Graphs, Monte Carlo Simulation, Evidence Based Attribution, and Explainable Risk Scoring, the platform demonstrates how advanced analytical technologies can strengthen national infrastructure protection and operational resilience. Developed by Randy Singh from Kalsnet KNet Consulting Group, the application transforms complex infrastructure relationships and incident evidence into actionable intelligence to support government leadership, infrastructure operators, and emergency response organizations.
